NASA says work on the rocket for Artemis Three has reached another milestone. According to the agency, teams at Kennedy Space Center in Florida have finished stacking the final solid rocket booster segments on the mobile launcher, and all four RS-25 engines are now installed on the core stage. NASA plans to roll the partial rocket out to Launch Pad 39B in the coming months for a fueling test.

A SpaceX cargo ship is on its way to the International Space Station. NASA reports that its thirty-fifth commercial resupply mission lifted off on a Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral on Tuesday morning, carrying more than six thousand three hundred pounds of supplies and hardware, including the station's final pair of roll-out solar arrays. The Dragon capsule is scheduled to dock on its own on Thursday.

SpaceX also has a well-traveled Starship back home. Space dot com reports that Ship 40, the upper stage that flew on Starship's thirteenth test flight in July, is now standing inside the Megabay hangar at Starbase in South Texas. It is the first Starship upper stage to return to its launch site after flying in space. It survived its splashdown in the Indian Ocean and was shipped back to Texas. SpaceX says it will provide first-of-its-kind data on a flown Starship heat shield, Raptor engines and avionics.

In business news, SpaceNews reports that the Czech defense and industrial company CSG has bought a nine and a half percent stake in Canadian space company MDA Space, becoming its largest shareholder. The Financial Post puts the value of the stake at about four hundred thirty-four million U.S. dollars, based on Monday's closing share price. CSG says the two companies could explore building manufacturing facilities in Europe together and partnering on acquisitions.

In Earth observation, Europe's newest ocean and land satellite is already sending home pictures. The European Space Agency and the Copernicus program say Sentinel-3C has returned its first images, with views of Italy, the Arctic and the Caribbean, and its radar altimeter has measured sea surface height. The satellite now begins about five months of commissioning.

And in startup news, Payload reports that Reflect Orbital has bought two launches from Indian rocket company Skyroot Aerospace. One will fly on the Vikram-1 rocket, which debuted in July, and the other on an upgraded Vikram-1U, expected to make its first flight in early 2027. The company did not disclose prices or launch dates. Reflect Orbital is developing satellites with mirrors that redirect sunlight to locations on the ground after dark.
