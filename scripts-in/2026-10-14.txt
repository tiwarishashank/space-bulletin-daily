NASA says work on the rocket for Artemis Three has reached another milestone. According to the agency, teams at Kennedy Space Center in Florida have finished stacking the final solid rocket booster segments on the mobile launcher, and all four RS-25 engines are now installed on the core stage. Next, crews will add the systems tunnel and join the core stage to the boosters. NASA plans to roll this partial rocket out to Launch Pad 39B in the coming months for a fueling test. While it is at the pad, the four Artemis Three astronauts will rehearse launch day and walk through their emergency exit route.

A SpaceX cargo ship is on its way to the International Space Station. NASA reports that its thirty-fifth commercial resupply mission lifted off on a Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral on Tuesday morning, carrying more than six thousand three hundred pounds of supplies and hardware. Among the cargo is the final pair of the station's roll-out solar arrays, along with experiments that include equipment to produce artificial retinas in microgravity. The Dragon capsule is scheduled to dock on its own on Thursday, and it will return to Earth with research samples in mid-November.

In Earth observation, Europe's newest ocean and land satellite is already sending home pictures. The European Space Agency and the Copernicus program say Sentinel-3C has returned its first images, with views of Italy, the Arctic and the Caribbean. Its radar altimeter has also measured sea surface height and the Greenland ice sheet. Spatial Source notes that the satellite now begins about five months of commissioning before it joins the Sentinel-3 fleet.

Weather forecasters in Europe have a new tool overhead. EUMETSAT says data from the infrared sounder on its Meteosat Third Generation sounder satellite entered operational service on Tuesday, as the spacecraft took the name Meteosat-13. The agency says it is the first instrument of its kind Europe has placed in geostationary orbit. Every thirty minutes, it can show how warm and humid the air is at different heights, and track ozone, dust and volcanic ash, which should help with earlier severe weather warnings.

And in startup news, SatNews reports that Satlyt has raised an eight million dollar seed round led by Houston-based Non Sibi Ventures. The company is building software to run artificial intelligence workloads directly on satellites made by other companies, linking their onboard computers into what it calls virtual data centers in space. Satlyt has offices in Sunnyvale, California and Nairobi, Kenya.
