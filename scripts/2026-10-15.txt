In Earth observation, a study in the journal Nature puts methane-tracking satellites to the test. Stanford University says researchers staged hundreds of controlled methane releases in the Arizona desert over a year. On clear days, satellites built to find methane detected ninety-two to one hundred percent of the plumes, while general-purpose land imagers caught only twenty-nine to thirty-seven percent. Clouds remained a major problem.

And in startup funding, Canadian company Mission Control Space Services says it has raised ten million Canadian dollars. The Ottawa firm, which builds rovers and autonomy software, says GreenSky Ventures led the equity investment, while Scotiabank provided debt financing. The company says the money will expand its rover production facilities and operations staff so it can take on larger missions.
